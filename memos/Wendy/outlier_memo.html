Research Memo: Assessing Outlier Influence on Communication Apprehension and Public Transit Usage

Author

LiangYingLee(Wendy)

Published

July 28, 2026

Research or Analytical Question

Are there any statistical outliers present in the data, and to what extent do these potential outliers exert leverage or influence on the fitted regression line between communication apprehension and public transit usage?

Data and Variables

  • Independent variable (\(X\)): Communication apprehension score (ca_score), derived from Likert items Q1–Q6 and Q13–Q18 (reverse-coding Q2, Q4, Q6, Q14, Q16, Q17).
  • Dependent variable (\(Y\)): Monthly public transit usage days (public_transit_days), mapped from Q26 using midpoint estimation.

Cook’s Distance Diagnostics

Answer, Response, + Summary of Results

To determine whether extreme observations distort the analysis, we evaluated bivariate regression influence using Cook’s Distance (threshold \(4/N = 0.0159\)) across \(N = 252\) complete observations.

Summary of Results

  • Key Finding: As shown in Table 2, we identified 7 influential observation(s) (\(4/N = 0.0159\)). Removing them strengthened the negative slope from -0.986 (\(p = 0.001\)) to -1.459 (\(p < 0.001\)), with \(R^2\) moving from 0.043 to 0.094.
  • Conclusion: As illustrated in Figure 3, removing high-influence outliers does not alter the core finding: communication apprehension has a statistically significant negative relationship with transit usage. Removing outliers reveals a stronger underlying negative effect.

What questions or uncertainties remain?

  1. Measurement Limitations: Converting ordinal category choices into exact monthly days via midpoints creates discrete cluster bands. Would Logistic Regression (predicting binary regular usage) better accommodate these extreme boundary values?
  2. Omitted Variable Bias: The identified outliers may reflect unmeasured socio-demographic factors (e.g., vehicle ownership, transit access, income) rather than true anomalies in communication apprehension.
